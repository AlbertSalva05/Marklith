How extraction works

marklith reads text with Tesseract, an open-source text recognition engine that runs inside your browser. The first time you extract, the browser downloads the engine and English language data from this site. Your image is never part of that download and is not sent anywhere.

Large photos are scaled down before reading so it stays responsive on phones. The original image is kept for display.

What is stored and where

Saved extracts (image, text, category, tags and dates) are stored in this browser's IndexedDB. Your theme and default format are stored in local storage. There is no account and no server copy, so your library does not sync between devices.

To remove everything, use Delete all saved data in Settings, or clear this site's data in your browser.

Limits of text recognition

Recognition is never perfect. Expect mistakes with small or light text, handwriting, photos taken at an angle, busy backgrounds and similar characters such as 0 and O or 1 and l. Words the engine was unsure about are counted above the editor, and "Next word to check" steps through them.

Formatting is a best guess from line breaks and spacing. Headings, lists and code blocks may need fixing in the editor.

To work, the page loads a few files from public services. These requests share your IP address and browser details with the provider, as every web request does. Your images and text are never part of them.

Everything happens in this browser, in five steps. Nothing is uploaded, and you can stop, correct or go back at any point.

  1. Step 01

    Add images

    Choose files, drag them in or paste a screenshot straight from the clipboard. On a phone you can pick from the camera roll or take a photo.

    • Up to 10 images per batch, 100 per extract
    • PNG, JPG, WebP or BMP, up to 10 MB each
    • Large photos are scaled down and compressed first
  2. Step 02

    Extract the text

    The text engine reads each image in turn and shows its progress in three stages: load the engine, read the text, format it.

    • The first run downloads the engine once; your image is not sent with it
    • Cancel at any time and keep your images
    • Images with no readable text are listed so you can retake them
  3. Step 03

    Review and correct

    The editor sits beside the image so you can compare line by line. On small screens the image, text and preview are tabs.

    • Words the engine was unsure about are counted; "Next word to check" jumps to each one
    • Place the cursor on a line to outline where it was read on the image
    • Reset to the extracted text if an edit goes wrong
  4. Step 04

    Choose a format and copy

    The same text is shaped three ways. The preview updates as you type and can show the formatted result or the exact text that will be copied.

    • Markdown: headings by level, nested and numbered lists, code blocks and tables
    • Outline: every heading a bullet, with its content nested underneath
    • Prompt-ready: plain text that keeps its structure, without Markdown symbols
  5. Step 05

    Save and find it later

    Give the extract a title, a category and tags. The library searches titles, text and tags and filters by category.

    • Add more batches to a saved extract; new text goes to the end
    • Deleting can be undone for a few seconds
    • Everything is stored in this browser only

A slab m, cut like stone, whose last leg splits into lines: the moment an image becomes text.

Principles

  • Content first

    The screenshot and its text get the space. Panels are divided by thin rules, not floating cards.

  • Say it in words

    Every state has a visible label. Nothing depends on colour, icons or motion alone.

  • Calm motion

    Short fades and slides, 120 to 160 ms. All of it switches off when reduced motion is on.

  • Easy to reach

    Targets are at least 44 px. On phones the main action sits in a bar at the bottom.

Palette and measured contrast

Navy ink for everything you read, one blue for everything you act on, and cool neutrals between them. Select any step in a ramp to copy its hex value. Ratios are computed from the hex values.

Elevation and radius

Flat by default. Panels sit on thin dividers; only things that float above the page get a shadow.

--radius-none0Workspace panels and dividers.
--radius-sm2pxButtons, fields, chips, tabs.
--radius-md4pxCards, dialogs, toasts. The ceiling.
--elevation-overlayone level onlyMenus, dialogs, toasts and the back-to-top button.

In the dark theme the shadow gains a 1 px ring so floating surfaces still separate from the page. High contrast darkens every divider to full ink.
